# Scientific papers and experimental decisions — BridgeCaption ES↔EN v2

Checked November 20, 2026. The papers below are *external hypotheses*, not measured gains on BridgeCaption. Reported impacts are cited only for their original study populations and systems.

| Paper (year) | Findings and limits | BridgeCaption experiment, stage, risk |
|---|---|---|
| Lin, Martins & Schuetze, [A Recipe of Parallel Corpora Exploitation for Multilingual Large Language Models](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-naacl.225/) (2025) | Their multilingual LLM study found noisy pair filtering important; around 10,000 clean pairs could compete with larger data. Tested LLMs, not our 78M-parameter Marian translator. | A/B exact+heuristic filtering on official licensed MASSIVE **training** rows. Risk: filter deletes valuable informal speech; preserve broad-domain replay. Medium CPU. |
| van Noord et al., [Quality Beyond A Glance](https://aclanthology.org/2025.coling-main.124/) (2025) | Human/manual review across corpora revealed high noise and quality differences; correlational evidence, varying language pairs. | Require source-identity/provenance, structural alignment, numeric consistency and review flags; *do not* certify semantic equivalence from overlap. Low CPU. |
| Meng, Wu & Monz, [How to Learn in a Noisy World?](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-naacl.416/) (2025) | Simulated misalignment reveals limitations of simple filtering and suggests token-level correction; method depends on reliable model confidence. | Hold advanced self-correction until a calibrated teacher and reviewed human examples exist. Don't use unverified model output as ground truth. High complexity, risk of self-reinforcement. |
| Rikters & Nakazawa, [Revisiting Context Choices for Context-aware Machine Translation](https://aclanthology.org/2024.lrec-main.1226/) (2024) | Correct context improves model outputs in studied MT settings, while shuffled or unrelated context can create misleading gains. | Test no-context vs valid past context vs shuffled context on *human-validated* multi-turn licensed dialogue. Never use future or English references as context. Risk: context leakage/latency. Medium CPU. |
| Agrawal et al., [Assessing the Role of Context in Chat Translation Evaluation](https://aclanthology.org/2024.tacl-1.69/) (2024) | Chat translation metric judgments can change when context is included, especially reference-free/out-of-English; not a reason to trust AI scores as ground truth. | Prepare context-visible, source-speaker-aware blind bilingual review packets; contrast model conditions with the same history. Low CPU. |
| Kim, [Context Is Ubiquitous, but Rarely Changes Judgments](https://aclanthology.org/2025.wmt-1.5/) (2025) | Human context-use judgments show low agreement and methodological complexity. | Predeclare contexts/categories, double blind bilingual scoring and agreement, avoid treating a hand-picked small list as generalization. Moderate human cost. |
| Liu & Niehues, [Conditions for Catastrophic Forgetting in Multilingual Translation](https://aclanthology.org/2025.mrl-main.23/) (2025) | Model/data scale matters; parameter-efficient fine-tuning alone does not guarantee forgetting resistance. | Compare base, parent and adapted systems across matched general, context and slang DEV samples. Keep rollback; replay MASSIVE train-only. Low-medium CPU. |
| Scholz et al., [Fine-Tuning LLMs for Translation: General Forgetting Mitigation Does Not Preserve MT-Specific Instruction Following](https://aclanthology.org/2026.wmt-main.36/) (2026) | Preservation of general LLM abilities can diverge from translation-specific behavior. Different model architecture from ours. | Separate adequacy/register/terminology tests instead of accepting general BLEU as evidence of conversational robustness. |
| Serdioukova & Zhilko, [AQI: a composite human-calibrated metric](https://aclanthology.org/2026.wmt-main.37/) (2026) | Automatic metric ensembles on WMT25 do not reach the human agreement ceiling. | Automatic metrics for mining and screening only; bilingual humans required for 95% claim. Large learned QE may exceed free CPU budget. |
| Thellmann et al., [Quantifying the Impact of Translation Errors on Multilingual LLM Evaluation](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1916/) (2026) | Span alignment with expert MQM judgements is challenging, and target translation errors matter. | Prioritize severe negation/entity hallucination errors for annotation; do not accept self-assessed labels as human ground truth. |
| Vernikos & Popescu-Belis, [Don't Rank, Combine!](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.653/) (2024) | QE-based hypothesis fusion improves studied multilingual models, but demands a quality-estimator and additional generation cost. | Defer runtime reranking until measured low-latency gains with a permitted open-weight QE model; likely unsuitable for CPU live captions now. |

## Decisions

1. **No new noncommercial subtitling data.** Previous Samuel & Audrey trained adapters are preserved strictly for research. The v2 production-compatible lineage begins from the previous MASSIVE stage-3 checkpoint, not from the NC round-10 adapter. This is a rights boundary, not a claim that the checkpoint reset from zero.
2. **No invented 100,000 parallel pairs.** Data growth is conditional on verified permissive rights, English/Spanish semantic alignment and provenance; unlabeled/synthetic examples are *not* independent natural conversational data.
3. **Do not re-score FLORES devtest.** The old 256-row subset was used once by a prior research branch. It cannot be a new untouched test.
4. **Human evaluation is an external prerequisite.** Prepare fully blinded, private, group-disjoint reviewer packets and tamper-evident sealing code, but do not fabricate reviewers or pass rates.
5. **Stress tests are not the final holdout.** Agent-authored illustrative slang cases may be used in development tests (explicitly synthetic), never described as independently unseen naturally collected conversation.

A literature review cannot establish per-method improvement on this repository without completed training and independent validation.
